# Dynamic transfer experiment, specified before response scoring

Question: can a support field inferred from static bending predict gauge strains under another, oscillating load? Separate force calibration, excitation frequency and soil model. No dynamic strain is an inversion input.

1. Linear transverse bending of the same 100 m clamped pipe on bilateral springs. Prescribe line mass 400 kg/m, constant distributed viscous coefficient c=2 zeta sqrt(m k0), zeta=0.02, k0=18 MN/m2. These are numerical test inputs, not field measurements. Use complex exp(+i omega t), consistent mass, and 0.2 m rotation-difference gauges. Reference excitation: vertical force, 1 kN total over a 4.3 m footprint centred at +6 m. Additional imposed foundation motion: u_g=1 mm cos^2(pi x/40) for |x|<20 m, zero outside, with harmonic time factor; use (k+i omega c)u_g and zero imposed displacement at clamps. This is a localized, spatially varying base motion, not an earthquake record.
2. Analytic uniform simply supported sine-load response, including exact finite-gauge strain and phase; independently assembled scikit-fem Hermite solutions for all four continuous loss profiles on a finer grid; zero-frequency and mesh convergence checks. Newmark average acceleration verified against analytic/modal or independent frequency solution and step halving. For verification use spatial complex L2 over 61 gauges from -12 to +12 m; do not discard imaginary parts. Target <1% for matched reference and <1% fine vs finer reference, <2% time-step change (report failures and refine before interpretation). Test boundary length or explicitly treat ends as a finite fixture.
3. Use the primary strain data (20 locations per load, 5 microstrain noise), four profiles, tau=1% and 10%, six original seeds, four load treatments = 48 matched datasets, 192 fields. Apply the v3 admissible zero-field and extended-endpoint selections before inherited selections. Preserve original key, method, status, coefficients and source hashes. Include all fields, including incompatible fits. No re-estimation. At f/f0 = 0.25, 0.75, 1.00, 1.15 with f0=sqrt(k0/m)/(2pi), predict both excitation channels, compare with independently assembled fine grid with prescribed continuous loss. Per row E=norm(pred-ref)/norm(ref), across all gauges; tabulate medians and within-seed paired differences. Six seeds describe these repetitions, not population inference. Selected illustrative transient: fixed seed 1101, asymmetric profile, tau=10%, same force footprint, smooth pulse sin^2(pi*t/T) for 0<t<T, T=1/f0. No claim of field validation or unique support recovery.
4. Separate dynamic model-discrepancy benchmark: coherent elastic layer (50 and 200 MPa), intact and asymmetric interfaces, same finite depth, width, BC and static intact-state equivalent stiffness as v3. Include soil inertia (rho=1800 kg/m3), 400 kg/m pipe. Compare complex mobility/strain with corresponding *intact-calibrated* local spring model; use the same nominal Rayleigh damping targets at 2 and 20 Hz (2%) in both model families for this benchmark. Force channel at 2, 5, 10 and 20 Hz. Matched refinement/finite-domain boundary sensitivity at least for one condition in each modulus; distinguish numerical convergence from physical model mismatch, no claim of half-space impedance. Report all results including near-resonant or boundary-sensitive conditions.
5. Keep raw gauge responses, metrics, verification and dependency versions. Do not change cases or metrics based on advantages seen in results. Record implementation deviations and their reasons. No dynamic inverse, speed-up, physical calibration or real-earthquake validation is claimed.
